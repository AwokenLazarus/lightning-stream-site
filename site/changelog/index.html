0.2.3

2 November 2026Latest

Usability, from Mike's hands-on run of 0.2.2 on Umbrel. The wallet UI, the VPN service and the signer change (one read added; payments are unchanged).

  • Buy and Sell. The main menu's Services is now Buy, beside Sell. Settings keeps Setup, Your node, Spending rules and Keys and backup as tabs; Records, Stream relays and Old channels are under Advanced.
  • Get started on Home. A list of what is left (approval key, node, starter rules, a shop, a first purchase), with the next step's button. The demo shop is added and allowed in one press, a service's page says when its shop isn't allowed yet, with the button, and a shop is named by its listing everywhere.
  • Real money in the same signature as the rules. On mainnet, signing the starter rules carries one box, "I understand the app pays with real money, within these rules", which writes the same confirmation Your node writes; no separate page. Your node can still turn paying with real money off and on.
  • One figure for what a payment cost. Paid is what left your node: the shop's amount plus the routing fee, from the wallet's settled record, with its two parts ("903 sats (900 + 2.009 fee)"). Wallet, Live connections and the visit bar name the same three figures: paid, used and credit left. A booking before a payment settles is shown as reserved, never as paid.
  • Credit you have with a shop. After a connection closes, what it left with the shop shows on Sellers, on the shop's service page and on the Wallet's row. A visit that runs on earlier credit says how much is left.
  • One Stop. Stopping a visit also closes its connection with the shop, unless another visit to that shop is still using it.
  • Allowed means paid. A shop allowed through its Lightning node's line in your rules is shown as allowed. Before, Sellers, AI and Spending rules said it couldn't be paid while the wallet paid it. A node line is shown as the shop's Lightning node, not as a coin address. A shop with no node on file gets a one-press fix.
  • VPN exits. Every exit the VPN service finds is listed, also a paid exit whose country isn't checked yet. Before, a paid exit such as the Pi Demo's (SV, 1,000 sat/GB) was found but never shown. Found on Nostr on the VPN page lists the exits your relays carry, with Add this exit; your devices then prefer it whenever it meets their rules.
  • Hashrate. A shop that goes Private is paid at its key address. Before, the hashmaker went on publishing the old web address and every rental failed at its first piece of work.
  • Plain words and fewer keys. Refused payments in Wallet activity are explained as the refusal pages explain them. A shop is named by its name on the service and visit pages and in Wallet activity. Your node's raw status is under Technical details. The AI address is also given by the box's IP address.
  • Faster first pages. Found on Nostr, Stream relays, VPN and Hashrate are asked once at start, in the background. The signer's slow-changing reads now serve pages for 20 s (they were kept 3 s, too short to help).
  • Also: the agent access key is shown only after Show the access key; starter rules' least node limit is 1,000,000 sats; Setup counts each seller and step once; Live connections shows a rate only while streaming.
  • The look of lightning-stream.com. The app is dark by default, with the website's near-black ground, lightning-yellow main buttons and amounts, white-blue accents, its Inter type and its mark. A light theme in the same colors is one button away in the bar, and the browser remembers it. The background's electric river is slower and finer, in yellow and electric blue, still reacts to the pointer and to clicks, and stays still with reduced motion and on low-power devices. Every page works at phone width.

0.2.2

2 November 2026

A fix release for a first purchase, from a tester's hands-on run of 0.2.1.

  • Sellers by name. Sellers, Spending rules, Wallet activity, Live connections, Found on Nostr and the visit pages name a shop by its listing ("Lightning Stream Genesis"), never by its key or its made-up ….shop.invalid address. Found on Nostr marks shops already on your list.
  • Who may be paid is a list. Spending rules show one row per seller: name, key or address, its Lightning node, and Remove. An edit can no longer cut a seller's node off; before, a long line wrapped in a text box and editing it could drop the node, after which every payment to that seller was refused. A seller with no node, or with a node other than the one the shop vouches for, is warned about before you sign. Removing a seller from Sellers points you to the rules where it is still allowed.
  • No blank pages. When the wallet won't pay (a seller not allowed, no route, a limit or cap, your node over "The most your Lightning node may hold"), the app shows why in plain words, what fixes it, and links, with the wallet's own words under Details.
  • Your node can't send. Before opening a shop's app or downloading, and on Browse, the app warns when your node's channels hold too little you can spend (after the channel reserve), with how much and what to do.
  • A visit's numbers. The visit bar shows what was used, what was prepaid and what is left as credit with the shop; Stop says what the visit used. The limit field says the least a shop needs (559 sat for a 546-sat ticket) and starts at 1,000.
  • Version. The app's version is in the footer and on Setup.
  • Faster pages. Found on Nostr, Stream relays and Hashrate show what they last knew at once and ask the relays and the hashrate service in the background; repeated reads from the signer within a few seconds are answered once. Each response carries a Server-Timing header.

0.2.1

2 November 2026

A fix to 0.2.0.

  • On Browse, Found on Nostr, a shop reached by its key (such as the demo shop, Lightning Stream Genesis) now offers Add this shop. Its list is signed by the key that is its address, so it is the shop's own. Before, it offered only Check with the seller, which asks the seller's web address; a shop reached by its key has none, so the check failed with a name-lookup error. Adding the shop by its key under Sellers worked all along.
  • A file that costs less than a shop's smallest ticket can be bought. A Download, or a file opened in your player, now lets its first call buy one whole ticket (546 sat, 559 with routing fees, at the least) even when the file costs less, such as Big Buck Bunny at Lightning Stream Genesis (about 284 sat). What the file doesn't use stays as credit with the shop. Before, the call's limit was the file's own price, so a buyer with no credit got a raw JSON refusal and nothing was paid. Where the wallet names a larger smallest ticket (up to 2,000 sat), the call is made once more with it, and any other refusal is now a page in words. Your spending rules still apply to every payment.
  • A web-app visit needs a limit of at least 559 sat. A smaller limit opened a visit whose first request said the limit was reached with nothing spent. The form now refuses it, says why, and sets the limit to 559 sat for you to confirm; what a visit doesn't use stays as credit with the shop.

0.2.0

1 November 2026Release on GitHub

A public test release. What is new since 0.1.19, in short; the sections below give the details.

In 0.2.0

  • Hashrate rental with masked jobs. The Stream Gateway (DATUM-compatible) now comes with the app on Umbrel, StartOS and Linux, and a renter's rented hashrate goes through it. Every job is masked with a key the gateway holds, so a rented miner can't tell which share is a block and can't withhold one. The renter checks each job before its miners get it: a rental whose job can't be shown masked ends, and nothing is paid before the first job has been checked. It mines solo, paid to your own address, or on Lazarus Pool, which takes jobs masked with your gateway's own key; a pool that doesn't is refused.
  • Hashrate payments. Both sides prove every share, and a renter pays as the work arrives: you never have more paid for and not yet received than a minute of the miner's hashing, or one share, one slice and one ticket (546 sat at least), whichever is more, plus any start fee shown on the offer. Shares sent again after a link drop still get their verdict, and a hashmaker's difficulty requests go no further than the renter.
  • Rentals. A start fee asks for 30 pieces of work at most, so a rental with a small piece starts before its first payment is due. A seller's listing never offers 0 miners just after a start or a new price. A Rent sent twice starts one rental and pays one start fee. Delivery records are shown as claims anyone can publish, and a flood of them no longer hides a hashmaker's own. A hashmaker's public summary names no renter.
  • Wallet limits. The hashrate rental's own limits apply only to the app's renter paying a rented miner's work; any other call that names the task "hashrate" is refused (ticket_rental_payer) and pays nothing. One task's credit is never spent by another: ticket sessions are per task, so a shop may see two sessions from one wallet.
  • Paying. Slow routes, channel drops of up to 8 s, a seller's 408, resumes from a byte (with If-Range), and stream tickets under load no longer end paid calls or pay twice. An agent's long paid call waits for the wallet instead of failing at the MCP. A seller behind a private channel is paid through its route hints. A file resumes after a channel outage. Wins are paid in the order their sellers' grace ends.
  • VPN exits. A tunnel pays about one and a half tickets ahead at most, asks again for a ticket refused for a moment, and shows what it has used as it runs.
  • The lab checks now require usage times the listed price, to the msat, on both sides for files, web apps and VPN tunnels.
Licenses · 1 change
  • The Stream Gateway image (xbt-datum-gateway) is CONVOY's datum_gateway (MIT) with Lightning Stream's changes. It is linked statically with libsodium (ISC), jansson (MIT), libcurl (curl license), musl (MIT), and two LGPL libraries: libmicrohttpd 1.0.1 (GNU LGPL 2.1 or later) and argp-standalone 1.5.0 (GNU Library GPL 2 or later). The image carries each license and a NOTICE in /usr/share/xbt-datum-gateway/. You may rebuild the gateway, or relink it against modified versions of those two libraries: this repository holds its complete source, the pinned library versions with their sha256 sums, and the script that builds and links it (scripts/datum_gateway_build.sh), at the commit the image's tag names. The GitHub release v0.2.0 carries the exact source tarballs of both libraries that the image was built from, with their checksums (SHA256SUMS-datum-gateway-lgpl).
Shops without a web address · 11 changes
  • A shop can be reached by its key alone, over an encrypted direct link, with no hostname, Cloudflare or port forward. The shop's node vouches for the link, and buyers check that before sending anything. Where the two ends can't connect directly, a relay carries the traffic, and it never sees what is sent. In private mode a shop uses relays only, so buyers never learn its address.
  • The relay is built into Stream, off by default. Shops announce the relays they use, and buyers choose among them.
  • A shop reached only by its key can list web apps and Files from the Sell page.
  • The AI address and agents can pay shops reached by key.
  • The AI page shows prices for shops reached by key.
  • A seller added, updated or removed on the Services page reaches the AI address at once.
  • Two AI sellers with the same name are told apart by their shop key or address, so preferring or blocking one no longer hits the other.
  • After a restart, the AI address finds sellers' models within seconds, instead of answering "model not found" for about five minutes.
  • A shop saved before its first service is now published on Nostr, so buyers can find it.
  • A web app such as Jellyfin works through a relay over a slow path (a VPN, a far-away buyer); the wallet keeps a buyer signed in and re-asks a price only when needed.
  • A shop reached through relays serves on four keys, each with its own relay connection, so its relayed buyers share several connections instead of one.
Sellers' own web apps · 9 changes
  • A web app that keeps buyer accounts opens when the buyer's session began on another of the shop's routes, or the shop has restarted: the wallet says who it is and asks again, instead of showing a raw 403. A buyer who stays anonymous at a shop stays so on all its routes and is told why such a page is refused, with nothing charged for that request.
  • A seller's whole web app, for example Jellyfin with its full interface, can be sold through Stream, paid as it is used, with an account made for each buyer.
  • The built-in player is gone. A media server is shared as a web app, and a new Files service shares a folder: buyers pay per GB to download a file or open it in their own player.
  • On Umbrel, Files shares a folder from the box's Downloads, which the Files app shows; read-only.
  • On StartOS, Files can share a folder from File Browser, read-only; File Browser stays optional.
  • On StartOS, a shop reached directly through the app can be bought from: Sell asks for a third key, link signing (message:write only), so the node vouches for the shop's link. Before, no buyer could allow such a shop. A link signing key pasted on a running shop is used within a minute, with no restart.
  • Saving the shop with nothing changed no longer restarts it (it cut buyers' sessions), even on a later day.
  • A shop switched from being reached directly to a web address no longer keeps serving its direct link.
  • Files from File Browser shares only File Browser's own folder, including the files you upload to it (File Browser keeps those readable by its own group only; Stream reads them through that group).
Paying · 40 changes
  • A download cut short by a channel outage now picks up from the byte it stopped at. If the seller has forgotten the session or has no room for more tickets, the wallet opens a fresh session once and carries on. It never retries a session the seller faulted or one you stopped, and never pays twice for the same bytes. If a seller ignores a byte range and sends the whole file, the wallet closes at once; the unread part costs at most what was already on its way.
  • A channel outage is no longer taken for a full route. Before, the payments that failed while your channels were down halved the tickets your wallet holds with that seller, for this session and the next, for up to ten minutes. Now the wallet waits 30 s and pays from prepaid tickets meanwhile, then holds as many as before. A route that really is full is still counted as before.
  • On a busy wallet, lottery wins are now paid nearest-deadline first. A seller's unpaid wins are hurried as soon as it reaches its limit. Every invoice is checked again just before it is sent, so none goes out about to expire. A 0.2.0 wallet tells a 0.2.0 seller it can cancel, and gets win invoices of up to 180 s, so a busy wallet no longer misses wins. Older wallets and sellers keep today's behavior.
  • A seller can now say how long it waits for the next ticket, and the wallet ends its own pause a little before that (a tenth of the wait, between 1 s and 3 s), so no ticket is ever paid into an answer the seller has already closed. A seller that doesn't say keeps today's behavior.
  • A slow wallet no longer blames a seller whose web server timed out waiting for the wallet's request (HTTP 408). Nothing was served or charged: the wallet sends the same request once more, and if it times out again, that call alone is refused and the session goes on.
  • A file no longer gets cut because the wallet's disk is slow while it pays many sellers. With 50 or 100 ms added to each of the wallet's disk writes and 20 other sellers' wins queued, the win a seller was waiting for was paid after 19 to 30 others, and its file was cut in 9 of 12 runs. It is now paid after 1 to 8 others, and the file arrives whole in 12 of 12.
  • After the wallet's Lightning node restarts in the middle of a download, a win it could not pay at that moment is paid on the next call. The seller used to refuse that call and every later one for as long as the session lasted.
  • A channel that drops for a moment no longer ends a paid file or answer: the wallet waits up to 8 s for it to come back, and as long for a route to the seller (after a link further along the way was replaced, a node may find none for some seconds), rather than sending a payment that fails. A seller the node has no route to for good ends the call with ln_no_route within 8 s counted from after the node's checks, and holds up no other seller's payments. The route is asked for with the invoice's route hints, so a seller reached through a private channel (an LSP's, say) is found as its payment finds it, and waited for the same way; an invoice with blinded paths, which the route query does not take, is paid at once, as before. A channel that stays down still ends what is paid by tickets, with that reason; a file paid by draws may still finish, with the wins it could not pay listed unpaid.
  • A win whose invoice ran out while it waited its turn is paid on the seller's next invoice, rather than ending the file; a win that would arrive after its invoice ends is not sent.
  • Going on with a file from where it stopped (range) no longer buys the start of it again from a seller whose server ignores ranges. Until now the wallet read and paid for a whole body sent in answer to a range, "like any other"; now it reads none of it and says ticket_range_ignored (a range from the first byte, which is what a player asks first, is a whole answer: read whole, up to the call's max_sats, not cut at the range's last byte, so a caller of bytes=0-1023 with a large max_sats can pay for the whole body), with what the call cost (on a route priced by the byte, what the seller's socket took before the wallet closed it). A call that goes on from an earlier answer can name that answer's etag (if_range): a seller whose file has changed since sends the whole new file, which is not read either, rather than the rest of another file spliced onto the old start. The app's player link and the wallet's own player pass the player's If-Range on and the seller's ETag and Last-Modified back; a seek on a changed file is answered 412, with that reason.
  • A win whose payment failed no longer costs a file: the wallet asks the seller to cancel that invoice, and pays the seller's next one at once rather than waiting for the first to lapse. It asks the same for an invoice it held back because the win would have arrived after it ended. A seller on an earlier version behaves as before.
  • A paid download or film no longer ends when a loaded node or a slow route is late with its next ticket, or when the wallet's chain or Lightning node stops answering for a moment. The wallet waits up to 12 s for each ticket while the seller keeps the answer open, and releases nothing for an answer the seller has closed, or after the call's deadline. Where only the Lightning node's router was away, tickets it never got hold their places for up to two minutes, and the file ends with ticket_none.
  • A whole paid call now has one deadline of 50 s, and answers plainly within about 55 s, before its caller's 60 s, even if a node or the seller hangs: asking again then does not pay twice. Three things can still run past that: paying a prepaid ticket, which waits as long as the Lightning node's own timeout (60 s); the checks of the nodes that begin a call, each up to its own 30 s; and the wallet's background check of its tickets while the Lightning node hangs, which holds the ticket book up.
  • An answer priced by the second is charged for that wait, by the clock, and each ticket can wait: at 400 sat a second, an answer of 3,600 sat came to 4,800 sat with the seller's word held back to 11 s, and to 7,600 sat with its ticket 11 s past due.
  • A seller that keeps not showing the wallet's tickets, and a ticket stuck on its way for over 90 s, now end the session, and the reply says which. A stop ends the answer at once.
  • A small payment works from no credit at a shop: the wallet buys a ticket sized to what the call may be charged, while the call itself stays capped at that charge.
  • Stopping a call ends it at once: nothing more is booked or paid for that session once the stop begins, and a prepaid ticket or win paid without its proof now counts in what the call charged.
  • A file is never read past the size its listing shows. From a 0.2.0 shop, a file that grew after it was listed is refused before any of it is sent, costing nothing on a route priced by size, such as Files, and only the request's price on a route with one; one whose server states no size is stopped at the listed size. From an older shop, the wallet stops at the listed size and pays only for what was already on its way.
  • A download, film or AI answer streamed to you is no longer cut after 50 seconds while it is still arriving: it runs as long as it delivers, within its limit. A seller that goes quiet is stopped after 30 seconds, and any answer the wallet stops early now reaches your program as cut short, never as a smaller file that looks whole.
  • A new zero-conf channel carries payments once its funding confirms.
  • The price a shop shows in its listing is now the most your wallet will pay it: one that asks more than it advertised is refused before anything is paid. This covers web apps, Files, AI, hashrate and VPN exits.
  • A shop caught over-charging is blocked until you lift the block (with your signature); a shop that loses requests is held to what they could have cost; each shop has a daily cap. Existing wallets get a daily cap of 20,000 sats per shop until changed.
  • Several requests to one seller run side by side in one session, each exactly accounted, so a busy page no longer waits on itself. Whatever way an answer ends (whole, cut short, or reported late), the wallet believes the seller's charge by one rule, and a charge above it is a seller fault.
  • Several answers to one seller, cut or reported late, and AI replies cut short, are believed no more than they could have delivered; an AI reply never past its quoted price.
  • A busy service refuses new buyers before they pay; buyers it is already serving stay served, and a refused buyer is sold nothing.
  • On a slow route the AI address no longer gives up on a seller while the wallet is still paying it, and pays a second seller for the same answer: a seller's time to answer is counted from when its call is paid for (up to 90 s for the payment, LS_AI_PAY_WAIT_S). A seller that has the request and is then silent is no longer followed by another; whatever a failed call cost is booked to its AI key.
  • A lottery win paid over a slow route reaches the seller before its invoice lapses: the seller's grace, and with it a win's invoice, is 60 s by default instead of 30 s.
  • WebSockets (for example a web app's live updates) stay open for up to six hours, paid as their bytes pass, instead of ending with an ordinary answer's limit.
  • A paid WebSocket is charged no more than the limit the buyer set for it: the shop's gateway ends it there.
  • Higher default limits for a shop: 100 sessions and 512 connections. A shop behind a tunnel is limited as a whole, not as if all its buyers were one address.
  • A buyer whose first payment takes more than 20 s to reach a shop (a slow route) can now open a session: a session's first ticket invoice stays payable for 60 s, as every other does, instead of 20 s. A payment that still arrives after its invoice lapsed is refused, with nothing paid, and the buyer is told so instead of seeing a timeout.
  • A call's prepaid ticket (or lottery win) gives your node at most 60 s to pay it, whatever your Lightning timeout, so that web apps and Files show the wallet's answer rather than a timeout. A payment with no final answer by then no longer blames the seller: nothing more is bought from that seller for up to about 85 s, and your next call takes up the ticket's credit once your node shows it paid and the seller has credited it. A seller that keeps the money without crediting it is named in your wallet's history.
  • After a restart, a session that picks up the credit left with a shop holds tickets with it again at once, instead of holding none until that credit ran out and buying another prepaid ticket.
  • A prepaid ticket that settles after its call ended, or just as the wallet stopped, is shown to its shop, so its credit is there for the next call. A shop is told only after it has signed an answer for that session, so another on the same node, or one merely naming that shop's key, is not.
  • A cancel names the session's own tickets, and also a ticket whose secret never reached the shop, so a session that took over a shop's credit keeps its tickets and nothing stays held.
  • A shop that will not say what an earlier session left with it no longer has a new ticket bought beside that credit: the call is refused with a time to retry. After two minutes a new session starts, your wallet's history names what may be left, and the next session uses that credit first, oldest first.
  • Credit one task leaves with a shop is no longer spent by another task's calls, which could take that task past its own budget. The credit now waits for a call of its own task, for up to about 20 hours, and another task's call to that shop buys its own ticket.
  • A call of one task is no longer paid from a session another task has open with the same shop, which spent that task's credit and booked the tickets it bought to that task, past the calling task's own budget. Each task now has its own session with a shop: a second task's call opens one beside it and buys its own ticket. A call at another address of the same shop no longer stops another task's session, and is no longer held back by another task's prepaid ticket still on its way.
  • A kill or a lost answer no longer buys a second ticket when the first was paid: the next call waits for the shop to take it, for two minutes at most. Long answers open for over ten minutes are no longer cut by a second call to the same shop.
  • An agent's paid download or payment that takes longer than a minute no longer fails at the MCP with "Resource temporarily unavailable" while the wallet goes on paying for it. The MCP waits for a paying call's answer as long as the wallet works on it; any other call still gives up after B2_SIGNER_TIMEOUT. If a paying call's answer is lost (the wallet's signer stopped), the agent is told the call may have paid (signer_no_answer, request_sent: true) rather than given an error that reads as if nothing happened. A paying call still has a ceiling, 24 h 5 min as shipped (XBT_MCP_PAY_CEILING_S, past the longest the wallet keeps any answer open), and at most 14 are in progress at once, so a stuck signer cannot take every worker of the MCP: a ticket_stop is always served.
VPN exits · 12 changes
  • A priced exit's region check now runs for a buyer with no credit at that exit. Each check is charged at most 20 sats, and the day's checks share a budget that survives a restart.
  • Stopping a session waits for its open answers' last receipts, so a VPN tunnel's spend is reported in full; the VPN seller page shows what was charged.
  • A VPN tunnel at a high price pays about one ticket ahead, as other answers do, not three seconds of traffic: at 40 Mbit and 500,000 sat a GiB, a stopped tunnel left over six tickets with the seller. At a very high price a tunnel goes no faster than its tickets can be paid, as a file already does.
  • A VPN tunnel whose next ticket is refused for a moment (a seller slow to show it locked) asks again when it is due, rather than ending with credit left. A refusal that will not pass (a stopped session, a blocked seller, the owner's limits) ends it at once.
  • What a VPN tunnel has used shows as it runs, from the seller's signed counts, not only after it stops: at a low price "Used" stayed at nothing for a whole tunnel.
  • Sell or buy a VPN exit, paid as you use it: a buyer's devices go out through another box's internet connection over Stream, by the GB, with no account; exits are checked for the country they claim, an exit only its own shop's gateway can open, and exits that waste a buyer's money held off.
  • The bundled country table is readable again, so exits' regions are checked as intended.
  • The VPN page says why a device is blocked.
  • The VPN page shows which exit each device goes out through and what it has spent.
  • Paid VPN tunnels open: before, each one closed as it opened, after its ticket was bought.
  • Restarting the app no longer stops the VPN service opening paid tunnels.
  • A shop that sells only its VPN exit is listed, so buyers can find it by its key.
Hashrate · 44 changes
  • The hashrate task's own limits (its 200,000 sat budget, tickets up to 10,000 sat, 600 an hour, outside the wallet's day, week and per-shop limits) now apply only to the app's renter paying a rented miner's work. Before, any agent or MCP tool that named the task "hashrate" could pay any shop on them, past your wallet's limits and with no approval. Such a call is now refused and nothing is paid. The renter pays through its own connection to the wallet, which nothing else in the app can reach.
  • If you run the renter by hand (xbt-hashlink renter --signer …), or your app's parts are of different versions after an update, point --signer at the wallet's renter socket (run/rental/signer.sock), and update every part of the app together: a renter on the wallet's ordinary socket now has every rental payment refused, and the Hashrate page says so.
  • Renting needs masked jobs, solo or through a pool, so a hashmaker can't keep back the shares that would be blocks. The app comes with its own gateway, the Stream Gateway (DATUM-compatible), which masks its jobs, and rents into it. The Rent page checks the gateway's jobs when it opens and before each rental; if they aren't masked (the gateway isn't set up or running, or the app is set to another gateway), it says so and rents nothing.
  • Your gateway on the Hashrate page sets the Stream Gateway up: solo to your own XBT address (the default), or through Lazarus Pool. The address is checked for your node's network first (bc1q, bc1p or a legacy address on mainnet). The page shows the gateway running, connected to your node, and masking its jobs.
  • Lazarus Pool mode masks its jobs too: Lazarus Pool takes gateway-held keys. A pool whose jobs aren't masked (pool-null) is refused for renting, and solo works. In that mode your gateway holds the key, so it could hide a block it finds from the pool, the same as any DATUM miner can today. Pools that hold the key themselves (CONVOY-style) come later.
  • The gateway's config holds no secret: the node's RPC credentials go to a cookie file only the gateway reads, its API has no admin password (every admin page is off), and it listens on 127.0.0.1 only. A block it finds is kept on disk even if the node's submitblock fails.
  • An app set to another gateway before this version is moved to the Stream Gateway once, and the Hashrate page offers the old one back. On armv7 the app has no Stream Gateway: the Rent page says the gateway isn't masking, and selling hashrate works as before.
  • Both apps check every share's hash themselves, from the Stratum lines alone. A share counts only if it proves its work; neither side takes the other's word, and a renter's verdicts set nothing.
  • Renters: paid as you use it, seconds at a time. You never have more paid for and not yet received than a minute of the miner's hashing, or one share, one slice and one ticket (546 sat at least), whichever is more, plus any start fee shown on the offer. That counts the credit left with the seller as well as the slices paid ahead: each ticket is sized to stay inside it. The app stops paying a seller after two first payments that delivered little.
  • No payment, not the start fee or the first ticket, until the gateway's own first job is shown masked. Before, a renter pointed at a gateway that doesn't mask could pay 5 times before the first job's check refused it.
  • Hashmakers: a renter gets no work it hasn't paid for. If it stops paying, your miners go back to your own pool within seconds. Measured on the real-money regtest: stopped with no work past what was paid. Hashmakers set no price by hand by default: it is the mining value at live difficulty.
  • Known limits in this release: a new price waits until the hashmaker's running rentals end, so while any rental runs no new rental starts at the new price; a rental's last ticket can pass its budget by less than a slice, which stays with the seller as credit (a ticket is never smaller than a slice); a renter that reads its link slowly can hold up a hashmaker's sends to it for up to 5 s each; each side keeps the hashes of up to about 4 million shares a rental, about 100 MB at the most.
  • Your gateway hides which shares win (xor_key), so a hashmaker can't drop the ones that would be blocks. The renter's page says, for your upstream, whether its jobs are blinded, checked job by job; one that isn't, or can't be checked, is refused. Measured: real masked blocks through a full rental, solo and through Lazarus's pool, each block's share one both sides proved; and over 10,240 shares a hashmaker's best guess at which win was at chance (balanced accuracy 0.498).
  • The app also ends a rental on shares held back, shares on an out-of-date job, or a pool turning most shares down.
  • Checking a share costs about 1.7 µs on a lab core, about 7 µs estimated on a Raspberry Pi 4.
  • Old and new versions refuse each other, with nothing paid: update both sides.
  • On mainnet the starter rules let the hashrate task pay a rental at market price: a budget of 200,000 sat, tickets of at most 10,000 sat, 600 payments an hour, paid by prepaid tickets only. These count against that budget alone, not the wallet's other limits. A wallet whose owner signed earlier rules keeps them until it signs new ones.
  • A trial slice and a rental smaller than the shop's ticket are paid from no credit, instead of being refused; credit left with a seller lasts about 20 hours, and the rental card says what left the wallet.
  • A rental shows "Connecting…" until its link opens; one that never opens says why, and that nothing was paid.
  • Rentals run over the encrypted link.
  • A hashtaker can buy a short paid test slice before renting, and a hashmaker publishes a signed history of what it delivered.
  • Miners behind one address (a home router, or Docker on Umbrel and StartOS) move between rentals once each way, instead of bouncing between connections under load. In a test of 25 rentals of 3 miners each at once, every rental received 98–99% of the work its miners could do in that time. Buyers pay a step ahead of the work and no more (see Renters, above).
  • A hashmaker rents out only miners that have connected and named themselves, so a rental never gets a silent connection that does no work.
  • After a break in the link, a rental keeps going even if a share's result was lost in the break. Before, the hashmaker stayed one share behind for the rest of the rental. With shares more than 3 seconds apart it refused the next slice and ended the rental as unpaid: the wallet was charged nothing, and the hashmaker went unpaid for that work.
  • A hashtaker that reconnects before the hashmaker has noticed the break carries on. Before, it was refused and the rental ended.
  • A hashtaker reconnecting over a link that has gone silent now gives up when its time to come back runs out, and its unused tickets go back. Before, it could wait for good with them still held.
  • A slice paid as the link breaks is taken while the rental waits for its renter to come back, within the rental's time to come back, and is paid and charged once. Before, a break of 2 seconds could refuse it and end the rental as unpaid.
  • A renter can no longer hang a hashmaker's whole app by asking for an impossibly high difficulty. A difficulty of more than four slices a share now ends that rental at once, whichever of its miners it was said to, with all of that rental's miners; other rentals carry on. That is above 131,072 at the default slice: above about 113 TH/s on a miner at 0.2 shares a second, or any miner if the renter's gateway starts there. Larger slices are the hashmaker's setting.
  • A renter's verdicts on shares no longer set what it owes: both sides count each share by its proof, so a renter that turns every share down still owes for each one proven, and the hashmaker forwards none past what's paid. Before, a renter that accepted a few shares could underpay without limit. See docs/P2P-HASH.md.
  • A hashmaker can no longer turn shares it held back into paid work by dropping the link and sending them again: a share sent again goes on to the renter's gateway for its verdict, aged as of the drop; as many as its miners' rate says could have been on their way count at once, and the rest only once the gateway takes them. A link that drops more than 8 times in an hour ends the rental.
  • After a dropped link both sides say their count once the shares sent again are settled, and the hashmaker's comes down to the renter's by no more than what it forwarded in the 2 s before the renter was last heard from, never more than 4 s before the drop. Before, a share sent again that the renter couldn't send on left the hashmaker's count ahead, and the rental could end unpaid.
  • A price saved with no rental going on holds no miner back: the hashmaker takes no new rental only until its shop charges that price, and the Sell page says so. While rentals go on, a new price waits for them, and the hashmaker takes it for new rentals the moment the last one ends. The shop starts charging a new price only once the hashmaker shows it has the new terms with no rental going on, so a renter taken at the old terms in that moment pays them to its end. A market price moving with the chain changes at most once every ten minutes. Before, a price saved on a fresh install held every miner back, under "a new price waits for the rentals going on to end", until the app's next minute check found the shop charging it.
  • Every share carries the renter's own id to its gateway, so its verdict is always read; a share with no id, or one still waiting for its verdict, is refused. A share whose time rolls back from its job's, or more than two hours forward, is refused. Before, shares with no id escaped the check of how many the gateway turned down.
  • A hashmaker with no fallback pool keeps its miners connected through a renter's wait for a payment on its way, and they go on with the rental when it lands. Before, it let them go, and a miner that doesn't reconnect was lost to the rental. A renter says a payment is on its way only when its ticket buys are measured too slow for the hashmaker's 2 s clock.
  • A slice's payment waits for the wallet as long as the wallet works on it. Before, the renter gave up after 200 s and took the slice as failed while the wallet might have paid it; a lost answer is now treated as possibly paid and never paid again.
  • A hashmaker can't change what a share is worth at the renter's gateway: mining.suggest_difficulty and other lines that aren't a miner's session go no further than the renter, and mining.configure goes on without minimum-difficulty. After a new difficulty, shares wait for a job checked as masked at it.
  • Sell, Hashrate says this beside "Rent out my miners", and the box starts unticked for an owner who has never saved the form; one who has keeps their choice. A rental card says in plain words why the hashmaker ended it for these two reasons.
  • Rent sent twice (a double click, or the browser sending the form again) starts one rental and pays one start fee. Before, each send started a rental of its own, with its own start fee.
  • An offer's delivery records say what they are: how many, from how many keys, and that anyone can publish them. No score is made of them and none is called confirmed. Before, the offer led with "TH-hours confirmed by both sides", which one person with two keys could fill at no cost.
  • Records about other hashmakers no longer hide a hashmaker's own. Before, 500 records about anyone, free to publish, pushed every other record out of view.
  • A hashmaker's published summary carries counts and totals only: rentals, slices and sats paid, and work. Before, it listed each renter's app key and the hour each rental ended, without the renter's say. It now goes out at most every six hours, dated to the hour; the rental-by-rental list stays on the hashmaker's box.
  • A rental's time runs from the first share your gateway accepts, so the seconds spent connecting and moving the miners, or a gateway slow to answer, no longer come out of it.
  • Mining solo, each offer says one machine's chance of finding a block in an hour, from your own node's difficulty, and how often a block comes at that speed on average. Through a pool it says nothing.
  • A hashmaker's listing more than two days old is no longer offered: it is from a hashmaker that has stopped publishing. Listings now carry an expiry other Nostr clients read too.

Paying

Security

  • Another page could make the wallet pay a seller you had allowed. A page served by another app on the same box (for example another Umbrel app), or on Linux any web page open in a browser on the same machine, could start a stream through the wallet. It stayed within your signed spending rules and only reached sellers you had allowed, but you hadn't asked for it. The wallet now answers such requests only by opening its home page, the player's stream links carry a token no other page can read, and the Linux connector refuses requests that come from a web page.

Fixes from reviews of the payment code

  • Two parts of the wallet could wait on each other forever when a refill overlapped a status check.
  • A shop that changed its ticket size could resize tickets still open after a restart, and drop prepaid ones with no credit.
  • A buyer coming back to a shop after a few quiet minutes could have their new tickets canceled. No money was lost (the payment went back), but the buy failed.
  • A payment note chosen by a program using the wallet could escape the per-session speed limit.
  • An error in cleaning up the wallet's records after a payment had gone through could be reported as a failed payment. For an on-chain send, a retry could then pay twice.
  • Tidying the wallet's ticket book could forget finished payments that weekly limits still count.
  • After a restart, a stuck payment or a restore, the wallet could undercount what it had spent, or send a ticket it had already marked failed.
  • A seller that never revealed its lottery draws could keep a buy open forever.
  • If the wallet's Lightning payment record could not be read, it was treated as empty and later overwritten. The wallet now stops paying instead.
  • The plain-words summary of a spending-rules change could hide a change inside a section, such as a seller's Lightning node being swapped. Every changed setting is now listed, and nothing is cut short.
  • Hot wallet: a coin already spent could be counted, a sweep the node had sent could be reported as failed, and a slow scan could hold up the wallet's status.

Other

  • The real-money confirmation no longer says the software has only run on test chains.
  • After a change that waits for a restart, the app says how to restart it on your platform (on Linux, docker compose restart in the install folder).
  • Linux guide: the first run includes the "Pay with real money" step, and a node that has only its cookie file gets an rpcauth line for the app.
